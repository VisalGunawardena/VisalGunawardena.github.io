    Why

    How water moves between plots today

    On most paddy tracts, water passes from plot to plot through cuts in the bund. Someone has to open each cut, watch the lower plot fill and close the cut again, often at night when rotation water arrives.

    Every cycle weakens the bund at the same spot. The flow is hard to judge by eye, so plots are over- or under-filled, and water that overshoots runs off the last plot in the chain.

    BundLink replaces the cut with a pipe that rests on top of the bund and decides for itself when to run. It uses only floats, a ball and a siphon. There is no power, no electronics and no hole in the bund.

    How it works

    Two rules, two settings

    Each unit carries water from one plot to the next. It runs only when both of its rules allow it, and each rule has one setting that you make once per season.

    Keep, in the upper field

    Never draw the upper field below its Keep level.

    A hollow ball sits on the inlet mouth whenever the upper field is at or below Keep. This protects the upper field's water and keeps the pipe full.

    Setting: slide the inlet head up or down its riser, marked in cm.

    Fill, in the lower field

    Stop when the lower field reaches its Fill level.

    A float tips a rocker that lowers a rubber cap onto the outlet as the lower field fills. The flow slows, then stops.

    Setting: the length of the float rod, marked in cm.

    Lower field below FillLower field at Fill

    Chain the units and a whole tract fills from the top plot down, then holds. After that, the canal only replaces what the crop and the soil take.

    Set Keep well below Fill for the same field, about 5 cm apart on steep terraces. The ball only lifts off a little above Keep (see sizing), and it needs that room to let water pass down the chain.

    Parts

    Inside one unit

    Listed in the order the water meets them. Everything except the ball, the float and the rocker is standard PVC plumbing from a hardware shop.

    1. Inlet cage and water-seal cupSits in the upper field. Water comes in through the mesh from the surface layer, away from the mud on the bottom. The cup around the mouth keeps the seat under water even when the field drops.
    2. Keep ballA smooth 15 cm hollow float ball on a rubber seat. At or below Keep it rests on the mouth, and the pipe's suction holds it shut. It lifts off a little above Keep, so it doesn't chatter.
    3. Keep adjusterThe cup, seat and cage slide together on the inlet riser. A scale in cm sets Keep.
    4. Siphon pipe and saddle63 mm PVC with long bends, resting on a saddle across the bund top. Nothing goes through the bund.
    5. Clear crown and prime capA clear section at the high point shows from the bund whether the pipe is full. The screw cap is where you pour in water to prime it.
    6. Outlet J-trapThe outlet dips into the mud and turns back up, like a sink trap, so the pipe end always sits in water.
    7. Rocker and rubber capA seesaw on a stake with a 2:1 ratio. A push rod on the short end carries the rubber cap over the outlet mouth; the long end carries the float rod.
    8. Fill float in a perforated tubeA 2 L float on an adjustable rod, inside a drilled 110 mm tube that keeps out weeds and calms waves. The rod length sets Fill. The same tube works as an AWD field tube.

    The hard part

    Why it stays primed

    A siphon only works while the pipe is full of water. Most siphons fail because air gets in when the flow stops, so the design deals with that first.

    • Both ends stay under water. The Keep ball seals while the cup still holds water, and the outlet's J-trap can't drain. A seal that leaks a little leaks water, never air.
    • Nothing passes through the pipe wall where the pressure is below atmospheric. Both valves sit at the submerged ends and act from outside the pipe, so there are no shaft seals to suck in air.
    • Running water sweeps bubbles out. At 10 cm of head the water moves at about 0.6 m/s, enough to carry small bubbles down the outlet leg. Any air that stays collects in the clear crown, where you can see it.
    • Re-priming takes about a minute. Clip both ends shut, top up through the cap, then unclip. At season start this is the only setup the pipe needs.

    Use

    A season with BundLink

    1. Prepare the land and form the bunds as usual. Install the units after the last bund pass.
    2. Set each unit across its bund. Push the inlet head into the upper field's mud and the float tube into the lower field's.
    3. Set Keep and Fill on their scales, for example Keep 3 cm and Fill 8 cm.
    4. Prime: clip both ends shut, pour about 7 L into the crown, screw the cap on, unclip.
    5. Walk away. When you pass, glance at the clear crowns. Full means working.
    6. Before harvest, lower Fill so the plots drain down. Then lift the units off, rinse them and store them.

    Bunds formed by a machine have a consistent profile, so one saddle size can fit every bund in a tract.

    Risks

    What could go wrong, and what handles it

    RiskWhat handles it
    Air gets in and the siphon stopsWater-sealed ends. The clear crown shows the problem from the bund, and re-priming takes a minute.
    Straw, weeds, snails and mudMesh cage, intake from the surface water, float inside a tube, and a full 58 mm bore with no small holes to block.
    Plots nearly level, so little head63 mm still passes about 1 L/s at 5 cm of head. Use 75 or 90 mm on flat land.
    Tractors, harvesters and cattleLifts off in seconds without tools. Low profile and staked feet.
    Heavy rainUnits close when the plots are full. They don't drain floods, so keep the usual drain outlets.
    Sun on plasticUV-stabilised pipe or a coat of paint, and storage indoors between seasons.
    Neighbouring plots with different ownersKeep protects the upper owner by design. Agree the settings together.

    Next

    Build and test plan

    1. Bench rig. Two drums or tubs at adjustable heights stand in for two plots. Measure flow against head, the levels where the ball and the cap close and reopen, seal leakage, and how long the pipe stays primed while idle. Aim for a week or more.
    2. Dirty-water test. Run muddy water with straw and husk through it for a full day, then check the seats and the mesh.
    3. Field pilot. Three plots in a chain for one season, beside a chain run the usual way. Record levels daily, farmer visits, re-primes and water used.
    4. Refine. Adjust the ball size, seat material, rocker ratio and pipe size from what the pilot shows.

    Pass marks for the pilot: levels held within 2 cm of their settings, no more than one re-prime per unit per season, and no bund cuts.

    Later

    Upgrades to keep in mind

    • AWD latch. A float rod with two stops and an over-centre spring makes the cap snap fully open only when the field has dried to about 15 cm below the soil, then snap shut at Fill. Each unit then runs alternate wetting and drying, the water-saving schedule IRRI promotes, without anyone reading a field tube.
    • Through-bund version. If priming proves a nuisance in the pilot, the same Keep ball and Fill rocker fit a pipe laid through the bund, which never needs priming.
    • Two sizes. 63 mm for most links, 90 mm for flat land and for the first unit of a long chain, which carries water for every plot below it.

    Before you present

    Protect the idea first

    Parts of this already exist on their own: float valves, siphon tubes for furrow irrigation, siphon spillways on dams, bell siphons in aquaponics, and float-driven canal gates that hold a constant upstream or downstream level, such as the AMIL and AVIS/AVIO gates. What could be new is the combination: a portable siphon that sits over a paddy bund, applies both a Keep rule and a Fill rule, keeps both ends water-sealed and is primed by pouring.

    Before the competition, search Google Patents and the records of Sri Lanka's National Intellectual Property Office, and talk to the university's IP office about filing. Showing an invention in public before filing can count against its novelty.

    Open questions

    • Should a unit pass storm water down when the upper field gets too deep? That would need a high-level override.
    • What should a unit cost? A parts quote for the first prototype will set the target.
    • PVC is cheap and easy to find, and HDPE survives knocks better. Which one for the pipe?
    • Who sets the levels when neighbouring plots have different owners?
